# Visual references for MCC-style maps (own-words notes)

Source: MC Championship fandom wiki (https://mcchampionship.fandom.com), screenshots viewed in-browser only.
Purpose: match the official maps' *scale, density, composition and quality level* in our own original maps.
These notes describe qualities and generic techniques only, not layouts to copy.

General observations that apply to nearly every game (cross-game "house style"):
- Saturated, toy-like palette: concrete, wool, terracotta and glazed terracotta carry most of the colour; very little "survival-grey" filler.
- Objective markers use the same visual language everywhere: a white/quartz pad framed by **yellow-black hazard stripes** (yellow and black concrete in alternating diagonal blocks).
- Every map is a sealed "diorama": either a box with painted walls, a floating island over void, or an arena with a stylised backdrop. Players never see raw terrain edges.
- Detail is dense at eye level (props, foliage, slabs/stairs, lanterns) but large surfaces stay flat and bright so players and team colours read clearly.

## Battle Box

Wiki pages used: https://mcchampionship.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_Box , https://mcchampionship.fandom.com/wiki/Classic_(Battle_Box) , https://mcchampionship.fandom.com/wiki/Canal_(Battle_Box) , https://mcchampionship.fandom.com/wiki/Splash_(Battle_Box) , https://mcchampionship.fandom.com/wiki/Campsite_(Battle_Box)
Screenshots viewed (7): classic arena overhead, canal arena eye-level, bazaar overhead, splash overhead, thermodynamics eye-level near centre, spaceship overhead, campsite overhead. No dedicated kit-room or spectator-box screenshot exists on the wiki; those notes are inferred from the arena shots and the wiki text.

### Visual notes
- **Scale:** small and tight. Each arena reads as roughly 25-35 blocks across, with a playable height of about 8-15 blocks (ground level plus one raised tier of platforms/rooftops). Two team spawns on opposite edges; the objective sits dead centre, so each team is only ~10-15 blocks from the middle. A 4v4 fight is meant to collide within ~5 seconds.
- **Enclosure:** fully enclosed box. The arena is surrounded by tall walls and, in several maps, a ceiling. It is not open to real terrain.
- **Indoor/outdoor feel:** "outdoor diorama inside a room". The trick used repeatedly: the inner walls are painted as a **sky backdrop** (light-blue concrete with white block-clouds, sometimes a big pixel sun) so a closed box feels like an outdoor scene. Other maps go fully interior (factory/lab with lava pillars, a spaceship hull).
- **Spectator treatment:** some walls have large **glass viewing windows** cut into them, framed in quartz/stone, looking into the arena (spectator/kit-room style openings). Upper wall bands sometimes carry a bold checker/pixel frieze (red/white, yellow/white).
- **Lighting/time:** always bright, flat lighting; no darkness used as a mechanic. Lava, glowstone-like blocks, lanterns and sea lanterns give extra light.
- **Palette:** each map has one strong theme palette plus neutrals: sandstone/terracotta/greenery for a market; autumn orange leaves + white snow-like stairs + grey stone for a forest camp; orange/red lava + grey/black stone for a volcanic/industrial look; cream + teal + orange for sci-fi; bright primaries + grass + water for a park.
- **Materials:** heavy use of stairs and slabs to sculpt cover, walls/fences as railings, leaves and planted bushes as soft cover, water channels (slow movement), lava as no-go zones, trapdoors/lanterns as small detail.
- **Detail density:** high per square metre. Cover is never flat boxes; it is small buildings, planters, pillars, bridges, market stalls, crates, trees with sculpted canopies, statue-like rock spires. There are typically 6-10 distinct cover pieces per half, mirrored so the two halves are fair (rotational or mirror symmetry is clear in every overhead shot).
- **Verticality:** modest. 1-2 raised walkways or rooftops per side, reached by stairs; jump-boost/leaping items are designed around these.
- **Readability of gameplay elements:**
  - **Centre objective:** a 3x3 white pad framed with a yellow/black hazard-stripe border (sometimes on a short raised plinth), always visually the loudest thing in the arena. Fill blocks are team-coloured (originally wool, now concrete), so who is winning the centre is readable from anywhere.
  - Spawn sides are distinguishable by layout and team-coloured players (leather armour in team colours, nametags with team icons), not by heavy team-coloured architecture.
  - Hazards (lava, water) are big and obvious, never hidden.
- **Backdrop treatment:** painted-sky walls with pixel clouds, or themed interior walls; occasional giant pixel-art graphic (a sun, graffiti words) on a wall.
- **Generic techniques worth copying:**
  - Sky-painted interior walls (light-blue concrete + white block clouds) to fake an outdoor scene in a sealed arena.
  - Hazard-stripe framing on the objective pad.
  - Mirrored cover with themed props instead of plain walls; every cover piece "is" something (stall, planter, tent, crate stack, machine).
  - Glass windows in the arena walls for spectator viewing.
  - One theme per arena with a 2-3 colour palette plus neutrals.

### Mechanics summary (own words, from wiki text)
- **Format:** 10 teams of 4 (current era). Over the game, every team plays 9 rounds, each against a different opponent, so each team faces every other team once (round-robin; 5 simultaneous 4v4 matches per round, one arena instance per match).
- **Round length:** 1 minute. **One life** per player per round.
- **Pre-round:** players pick a kit (since the 4KO update via a kit selection menu; before that, by taking items in a kit room). A short prep phase precedes the gates opening (exact length not given on the wiki; typically ~10-15 s in practice, low confidence).
- **Objective:** fill the 3x3 centre with your team's blocks (originally team-coloured wool and shears; since MCC 26 team-coloured concrete and a pickaxe). Players can break enemy blocks in the centre.
- **Win conditions:** (a) fully fill the centre with your colour = instant round win; (b) if time runs out, the team with more of its blocks in the centre wins; (c) eliminating the whole enemy team leaves the survivors free to fill the centre (kills are not required to win). Equal centre count at time-out = draw (draws were removed for a while and re-introduced in MCC 33 "in a lesser amount", i.e. they still occur but rarely).
- **Scoring (current):** 15 coins per personal elimination; round win = 160 coins to the team (40 per member); draw = 60 to the team (15 per member). The Double Trouble remix (MCC 21) used 80 per team (20 each) for a draw and split win coins on ties.
- **Elimination:** since MCC 14, an eliminated player becomes a spectator for the rest of the round (earlier they were held in their team's kit room). Since MCC 8, no friendly fire and players cannot share kit items with teammates.
- **Kits (current 5-kit system, items vary per map):**
  - Healer - sustain (e.g. sword + bow/arrows + Orbs of Regeneration or splash healing).
  - Sharpshooter - ranged (bow or crossbow, extra arrows, sometimes Punch or a Spark of Speed / Leaping potion).
  - Gadgeteer - utility (crossbow/bow plus a harming/leaping potion, lingering harming, or similar).
  - Flanker - mobility (axe, often Knockback, 2-3 Sparks of Speed, sometimes TNT).
  - Scrapper - frontline/brawler (sword/bow + golden apple, or crossbow + firework rockets, or harming splash potions).
  - Example loadouts from the wiki: a park-themed map gave Healer stone sword + bow + 8 arrows + 3 regen orbs; Sharpshooter crossbow + Spark of Speed; Gadgeteer Punch-I bow + 4 arrows; Flanker stone axe + 3 Sparks of Speed; Scrapper bow + 2 harming splash potions. A later forest-camp map gave Flanker a stone axe + 2 Sparks of Speed + TNT and Scrapper a crossbow + 3 firework rockets.
  - Every player also has basic gear (team-coloured armour, the centre blocks and the breaking tool). The wiki says basic weapons vary per map.
- **Older kit system (MCC 2 to roughly MCC Rising):** everyone got a default loadout (iron or stone sword, bow, 8 arrows) plus **one bonus item** chosen in the kit room, e.g. crossbow + 8 arrows, Splash Harming II, Splash Healing II, Depth Strider III boots, Knockback II stone sword.
- **Custom items:**
  - *Sparks* - consumable: right-click to apply a short effect to yourself, or left-click an enemy to apply it to them (Speed, Regeneration, Levitation, Invisibility, Escaping).
  - *Orbs* - thrown, apply an effect in an area on landing (Regeneration, Levitation, Glowing).
  - *Timed Orbs* - thrown, apply the effect after a delay (Harming, Blindness).
- **Remixes:** Double Trouble (two capture points, Loyalty III tridents only); Map Rotation (a different arena each round); Duo Stack (teams split into 2-player stacks: the top player fights, the bottom player moves/places/breaks blocks, they share health and swap roles).
- **Notable history:** wool to concrete (MCC 26); spectator-on-death (MCC 14); no friendly fire / no item sharing (MCC 8); kit selection menu and removal of the per-matchup round summary (4KO); a new arena roughly every 1-3 events.
- **Confidence:** visuals: high (7 screenshots, consistent patterns). Mechanics: high for round count/length, lives, scoring, item types, history; medium for exact centre size (3x3 from screenshots) and default gear; low for prep-phase length (not on the wiki).

## Sky Battle

Screenshots viewed: 4 high-angle overviews of different maps (bright floating islands, autumn temple, city, dark space station).
- **Scale:** large. A ring of 10-12 team spawn islands around a central hub, overall diameter roughly 120-180 blocks. Each spawn island is small (~12-18 blocks across); the centre structure is the biggest and tallest piece (~30-50 blocks wide, 20-40 tall). Vertical spread ~30-60 blocks.
- **Enclosure:** fully open, floating over void, with a shrinking border as the pressure. Nothing at the edges except sky.
- **Outdoor/time:** mostly bright daytime sky with light, almost white haze; one variant uses a black starry void with red guide lines for a space theme.
- **Palette:** each map has a strong 2-3 colour identity (orange/red/white; terracotta and autumn orange with red roofs; grey/white with red accent cubes and coloured pipes; dark grey/black with orange, teal and red lights). Island tops in the theme colour with a contrasting rim.
- **Materials:** concrete and terracotta for colour, stone/deepslate for undersides, custom trees, quartz/white for architecture.
- **Detail density:** medium-high on every island (small hut or tower, a tree, a chest spot); high in the centre (rings of columns, walkways, towers).
- **Readability:** spawn islands are identical in shape for fairness; coloured paths/bridge lines between islands hint at routes; the symmetrical, ringed centre is always the obvious "middle".
- **Backdrop:** pure sky or dark void; no terrain.
- **Techniques:** radial symmetry; floating islands with sculpted inverted-cone undersides; one tall central landmark; small decorative floating chunks between islands to fill the void without blocking routes.
- **Confidence:** high.

## Dodgebolt

Screenshots viewed: 2 eye-level arena shots (two different seasons' versions).
- **Scale:** small court, roughly 20-25 x 12-15 blocks, sunk 2-3 blocks below a surrounding walkway and split down the middle into two team halves. Grandstands rise around it to 15-25 blocks.
- **Enclosure:** open-air stadium: court in a pit, spectators all around.
- **Outdoor/time:** daytime sky above; the stadium frames the view.
- **Palette:** each half of the floor is painted in that team's colour (team colour + white) with a big pixel-art logo in the floor; surroundings are stone/brick with colourful banners.
- **Readability:** the team colour IS the floor; a dark centre line divides the halves; players and arrows stand out against white. Huge pixel-art team logos/numbers on the stand walls.
- **Backdrop:** tiered bleachers full of spectators, giant pixel-art mascot statues on the roofline, team banners. This is the finale set piece and the most dressed-up backdrop in the event.
- **Techniques:** pit court with a spectator ledge; floor painted in the two finalists' colours per match; giant pixel-art mascots; bleachers.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Parkour Warrior (original, Survivor, Dojo)

Screenshots viewed: 1 original-version course shot, 2 Survivor-variant shots. No Dojo images exist on this wiki (Dojo is an MCC Island mode); notes for Dojo are not image-based.
- **Scale:** long linear courses. Original: a straight sky course of stages, each a cluster of jumps ~20-40 blocks long, several hundred blocks in total. Survivor: a chain of floating platforms linked by jump lines, segments ~15-30 blocks.
- **Enclosure:** open sky, floating; one-direction progress with the next landmarks visible ahead.
- **Outdoor/time:** bright day for the original; the Survivor variant uses a warm sunset (orange-to-blue gradient, low sun) for mood.
- **Palette:** original: white/quartz with bold magenta/pink accents plus neon stage colours. Survivor: dark wood, teal trim, autumn-orange trees, red lanterns (east-Asian garden look).
- **Readability:** checkpoint pads are distinctive framed/ringed pads; jump blocks contrast strongly with the void; tall thin vertical lines (beacon-like) mark stage ends; distant islands preview what's next.
- **Techniques:** big custom trees as focal points; railings and lanterns on platforms; one strong theme per course; islands at varied distances for depth.
- **Confidence:** medium (few images); Dojo: low.

## Rocket Spleef (original) and Rocket Spleef Rush

Screenshots viewed: 3 Rush maps, 1 original arena.
- **Scale:** arenas ~30-50 blocks across with 3-6 stacked layers spaced ~5-8 blocks apart; total height ~25-40 blocks. Players fly above with rockets/elytra.
- **Enclosure:** fully open, floating in bright sky; falling out = elimination.
- **Palette:** extremely bold. One style: flat single-colour concrete layers in rainbow order (each layer a different colour, so you always know your layer). Another: a two-colour checkerboard on every layer. Themed versions: white towers with blue domes, or a construction site with cyan glass pillars and wood floors.
- **Readability:** colour-per-layer makes depth obvious; irregular blob outlines make holes read clearly.
- **Techniques:** rainbow or checker layers; themed vertical structures whose floors are the breakable layers; small framed base platform at the bottom.
- **Confidence:** high.

## Hole in the Wall

Screenshots viewed: 3 maps (game-show hall, city rooftop, sci-fi lab).
- **Scale:** small square platform (~9-11 blocks across, raised) in the centre; walls sweep across it. The surrounding set is large (40-80 blocks) but purely decorative.
- **Enclosure:** the platform is open, but the set is an enclosed studio or a city view; often the platform sits high above ground (rooftop) for drama.
- **Palette:** studio: black/white checker floor, red/white trim, coloured grandstands. City: dark platform over a colourful city below. Lab: dark interior with yellow/black hazard trim and neon accents.
- **Readability:** neutral/dark platform floor with a clear border; the moving walls are solid bright colours (lime, yellow) so hole shapes read instantly.
- **Backdrop:** heavily dressed: big pixel lettering, bleachers with spectators, giant letters/symbols on walls, display screens.
- **Techniques:** game-show stage aesthetic; spectator stands; bright single-colour walls; raised platform with drops on all sides.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Bingo but Fast (and Lockout Bingo)

Screenshots viewed: 2 maps (snowy mountain, cherry valley).
- **Scale:** large natural landscape, roughly 300-500 blocks across, with terrain variety (peak, river, cliffs, biome patches). Players drop from a floating hub above.
- **Enclosure:** open world inside a border; natural-looking.
- **Palette:** vanilla biome colours with a theme twist (cherry trees everywhere, snowy peaks, nether mash-ups).
- **Readability:** a big floating crown-topped hub ship hangs above the map as the landmark; small built landmarks (e.g. a giant pixel-art shape on the ground).
- **Techniques:** compact, custom-shaped terrain packed with resources; one dramatic central feature; the hub visible from everywhere.
- **Confidence:** medium (terrain style clear; resource placement not visible).

## Grid Runners

Screenshots viewed: 2 rooms (bowling alley, canal/building). The game is a grid of many small themed puzzle rooms.
- **Scale:** each room ~15-25 x 15-25 blocks, ~8-12 tall; teams progress room by room through a large grid.
- **Enclosure:** cells with open tops; neighbouring rooms visible over walls.
- **Palette:** each room has its own theme; the dividing walls are white/light grey with dark trim.
- **Readability:** strong per-room theming; task objects are big and bright; the sidebar tracks rooms completed.
- **Techniques:** modular rooms on a uniform grid; consistent white frame walls so the grid reads as one structure; each room a mini-diorama.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## Railroad Rush

Screenshots viewed: 3 (two canyon/mesa shots, one jungle-ruin overview).
- **Scale:** long parallel lanes, one per team, each a ravine ~10-15 blocks wide and 100+ blocks long with walls 10-20 blocks high.
- **Enclosure:** semi-enclosed canyon lanes, open sky.
- **Palette:** natural but saturated: banded red/orange terracotta, grey stone ruins with greenery, snowy variants.
- **Readability:** rails are the gameplay element, laid over terrain that clearly blocks them (gaps, rubble, water); TNT effects are big.
- **Techniques:** layered terracotta strata on canyon walls; ruins and overgrowth as obstacles; identical lanes side by side for fairness.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## Sands of Time

Screenshots viewed: 1 (a seasonal snowy reskin of the central hall).
- **Scale:** a large central hall (~40-60 blocks wide, 30+ tall) around a giant hourglass, with tunnels branching off into the dungeon.
- **Enclosure:** fully indoor, temple/dungeon.
- **Palette:** in the reskin: dark blue-grey stone with purple and gold accents (the normal version is presumably sandstone/gold; not verified).
- **Readability:** the hourglass is a huge vertical centrepiece with coloured sand bands; exits are framed doorways around the hall.
- **Techniques:** giant prop as the timer; pillars and statues around the walls; arched tunnel mouths.
- **Confidence:** low.

## Ace Race

Screenshots viewed: 3 maps (sky fantasy, city/pool, snowy cave).
- **Scale:** long lap courses, several hundred blocks per lap, paths ~5-15 blocks wide, with drops and climbs of 10-40 blocks.
- **Enclosure:** mixed; open sky, indoor halls and caves alternate.
- **Palette:** maximal colour: giant pixel-art plants, fantasy castles, tiled pools, pixel signs. Snowy variant: white, grey stone, orange/red accents.
- **Readability:** speed/jump pads are distinct coloured blocks (red pads, patterned orange floor pads); big signage and arrows give direction; lava hazards are obvious.
- **Techniques:** oversized pixel-art scenery (giant flowers, vines); tiled pool walls; floating set pieces; contrasting pads.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

## TGTTOS (To Get To The Other Side)

Screenshots viewed: 3 maps (building facade, biome strip, construction/walls).
- **Scale:** each map is a straight run of roughly 60-150 blocks from start to finish, ~15-40 blocks wide. Very short rounds.
- **Enclosure:** floating strip in sky/void; sheer drops at the edges.
- **Palette:** one gimmick theme per map: a biome gradient (green to icy blue), a red-brick facade, a construction site in yellow/black hazard stripes with warning signage.
- **Readability:** obvious start and finish (gate, beacon/line); hazard stripes on obstacles; big signage.
- **Techniques:** the whole map as a floating cross-section "slice"; big pixel-letter signs; themed buildings as obstacles.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Big Sales at Build Mart

Screenshots viewed: 3 (mall interior with carts, build area, block palette sheet).
- **Verified:** indoor mega-mall with glossy white floors; multi-storey storefronts with bold red/white/yellow colour blocking; big hanging department signs with pixel-art icons and lettering, one per block category; minecart-style shopping carts that players ride; skylight/glass roof giving soft daylight.
- **Build area:** a grid of raised square plots (~7x7) with light stone/quartz borders, small corner lights, dark floor between plots; a finished reference build sits on one plot; a big branded shop sign on the wall behind.
- **Scale:** mall hall ~60-100 blocks long, ceiling 25-40 blocks; storefronts 2-3 storeys.
- **Departments:** colours (concrete + glass in all colours), garden (flowers, leaves), ores (mineral blocks), stone variants, copper, lumber, bricks, sand, nether blocks.
- **Techniques:** category departments with hanging pixel-icon signs; colour-blocked facades; cart vehicles; raised lit build plots.
- **Confidence:** high.

## Meltdown

Screenshots viewed: 2 (nether-fortress style, laboratory style).
- **Scale:** multi-room arena ~60-100 blocks across, rooms ~15-25 blocks, 2-3 floors.
- **Enclosure:** indoor/sealed; spreading lava/heat as the pressure.
- **Palette:** fortress: blackstone/basalt with gold and lava. Lab: white, light grey and blue with yellow/black hazard floor stripes.
- **Readability:** objectives (gold-block pedestals) glow on dark surroundings; hazard stripes mark danger; lava is unmistakable.
- **Techniques:** room-by-room heat progression; hazard striping; gold objective pedestals.
- **Confidence:** medium.

## Parkour Tag

Screenshots viewed: 2 maps (town plaza, red-roof town in a sky-painted box).
- **Scale:** compact arena ~40-60 blocks across, 15-25 tall, extremely dense with climbable rooftops.
- **Enclosure:** sealed box with the same painted-sky walls (blue concrete, white pixel clouds, pixel sun) as Battle Box, plus a large glass viewing window.
- **Palette:** one strong theme colour (red roofs and brick; or a pastel colourful town).
- **Readability:** rooftops/ledges in a contrasting colour (red slabs) read as routes; chimneys, awnings and balconies as stepping stones.
- **Techniques:** dense urban parkour playground with routes on 3-4 height levels; sky-painted walls; viewing window.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Foot Race

Screenshots viewed: 1 (desert-temple corridor course).
- **Scale:** corridors ~6-10 blocks wide, multi-level, long.
- **Enclosure:** indoor temple/crypt with openings to the sky.
- **Palette:** sandstone and terracotta with lime highlights and fire/lava accents.
- **Techniques:** sculpted sandstone walls with stair/slab detailing; colour-coded pads; fire as light and hazard.
- **Confidence:** low-medium.

## Survival Games

Screenshots viewed: 3 maps (modern city, biome field with central pavilion, dense novelty town).
- **Scale:** large, ~200-400 blocks across; many buildings 10-40 blocks tall; a tall central landmark.
- **Enclosure:** open world inside a border.
- **Palette:** varies; the city uses black asphalt with white road markings, colourful storefronts and street lamps. The field version has a purple/teal pavilion on sand with purple ground patterns around it.
- **Readability:** the centre (cornucopia) is the tallest and most saturated structure, with a ground pattern radiating from it; roads give natural navigation.
- **Techniques:** dense toy-town of novelty buildings (giant food, oversized props), signage on every shop, street furniture, a showpiece centre with event branding.
- **Confidence:** medium-high.

## Decision Dome (voting hub, not a game)

Screenshots viewed: 1.
- **Scale:** circular dome ~40-60 blocks in diameter, sunken centre.
- **Enclosure:** indoor dome with a dark shell.
- **Palette:** dark grey/black shell; each voting option is a translucent coloured stained-glass wedge (red, purple, blue, cyan, green, yellow...).
- **Readability:** each option is a coloured pie slice with stepped glass; the centre is a patterned platform with game icons on hanging displays.
- **Techniques:** radial pie-slice layout, stained glass for colour, hanging displays.
- **Confidence:** medium.
