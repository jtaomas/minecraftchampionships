# Map Profiles — Scale, Density and Feel per Game

Purpose: give each of our original maps the right *character and quality level* for its game, based on how the official event's maps are built. These are general characteristics, written in our own words. They are not layouts to copy. Design our own layouts.

**How to read the numbers.** Wiki sources give rules, timings, round counts and map counts, and these are marked *(sourced)*. Block sizes are almost never published. They are estimates from movement speed, record times and general knowledge of the broadcasts, marked *(est.)*. Treat estimates as ±30% targets and check them in playtests.

**Format assumptions.** The main event uses 10 teams of 4 (40 players). Many games run per-team copies or parallel arenas, so "footprint" below means one copy unless stated otherwise.

## Sky Battle
- **Rounds / maps:** 3 rounds of about 4 minutes, plus overtime. Roughly 10 maps exist in the rotation, often one per event *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** a circular field of floating islands over the void. Each team spawns on its own island on the outer edge, and the islands are spaced evenly around the centre. With 10 teams, plan a ring roughly 70–100 blocks from the centre, so the total field is about 200–260 blocks across *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** islands float high over the void, 60–100+ blocks. The border closes from the sides and the top, so there is a real "ceiling" that comes down. Some maps also add a rising floor. Plan 30–60 blocks of vertical play space at the start *(sourced mechanic, est. heights)*.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - The spawn island holds starter loot.
  - Between spawn and centre are "intercept" islands with better loot, placed where neighbouring teams meet.
  - The centre is a large, rich island or cluster with the best loot. It can sit lower or higher than the spawns, which changes how fights flow.
  - Ores and blocks are embedded in the terrain all the way from spawn to centre.
  - Players carry infinite building blocks, so the gaps between islands are meant to be bridged. Keep them 8–25 blocks so bridging is tense but quick.
- **Density / scenery:** medium-high. Islands are organic, sculpted terrain with themed structures on top. There is no scenery outside the playable ring: the void and the sky are the backdrop.
- **Themes (types):** seasonal and environmental (snow, desert/mesa, forest/hills), whimsical (candy), historical (medieval), urban/industrial.
- **Mood:** tense battle royale. It is quiet early on, then gets crowded and chaotic as the border closes.
- **What players remember:** the ring of colourful spawn islands, and the dramatic central island where the last fights happen.
- **Sources / confidence:** mcchampionship.wiki.gg and mccisland.wiki Sky Battle pages for the mechanics. The spacing numbers are estimates. Confidence: **high** for structure, **medium** for numbers.

## Battle Box
- **Rounds / maps:** two 4-player teams per match, spawning on opposite sides. Each team plays every other team, 9 rounds of 1 minute, one life each. The pool has 20+ maps *(sourced)*. Matches run in several identical arena copies at once.
- **Footprint:** small and mirrored. About 25–40 blocks across, where a sprint from spawn to the centre takes about 4–6 seconds *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** mostly one or two levels. Cover is 2–6 tall, plus an optional raised flank or catwalk. Walls or a roof enclose the arena.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - There is a kit-selection spawn room at each end, with five kits per map.
  - A **3×3 capture pad** sits in the exact centre and is filled with team-coloured concrete *(sourced)*.
  - The layout is symmetrical, with two or three lanes (centre plus flanks).
  - Cover is made of themed props.
  - Some maps place pickups near the middle.
- **Density / scenery:** very dense for its size. Each arena is a fully dressed miniature room or place, and the props double as cover. Almost no scenery sits outside the walls.
- **Themes (types):** industrial interiors, seasonal and holiday, everyday places (stations, campsites, markets), vehicles and ships, spooky.
- **Mood:** short, punchy, readable PvP.
- **What players remember:** the tight themed rooms and the scramble for the coloured 3×3 in the middle.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg and mccisland.wiki Battle Box pages. Confidence: **high** for format, **medium** for size.

## Dodgebolt
- **Rounds / maps:** the final duel between the top two teams, best of 5 *(sourced)*. There is one arena per season, re-skinned for Pride, Halloween, winter and other special events *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** a rectangular playfield split by a centre line into two halves that players cannot cross. A curved line near the middle disables the bow if a player steps past it. A rough size is 20–30 wide by 30–45 long *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** the floor is flat. Around it are tall crowd stands or bleachers and a mascot display above the arena *(sourced: spectators with banners, mascots above)*.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - Each team's arrow spawns at its own spot.
  - The floor shrinks inward as the round goes on.
  - The rest of the server watches from the stands.
- **Density / scenery:** the playfield is plain and readable. The surroundings are extremely dense: stadium, lighting, banners, team colours.
- **Themes (types):** sports-arena or championship stage, restyled each season.
- **Mood:** climactic, spotlit, high pressure.
- **What players remember:** the packed stadium around a small, clean court.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg Dodgebolt and Dodgebolt (Map). Confidence: **medium**.

## Parkour Warrior
- **Format:** the current version has 5 stages × 3 sections, for 15 main challenges. Each stage opens with an optional 3-section bonus path. At the end, players pick an easy, medium or hard finale, which sets the team multiplier. The run has a 10-minute limit plus 30 seconds of overtime *(sourced)*. The original version was a single brutal linear course and was retired as too hard. That is a cautionary example.
- **Footprint:** one long linear course. Each section is about 20–40 blocks, so the main path totals about 400–800+ blocks. Bonus paths branch off to the side *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** sections float over void or water with 10–30 block falls, and a fall sends the player back to the section start. The course climbs gently from stage to stage.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - Clear stage gates and a visible difficulty ramp.
  - Bonus paths sit visibly beside the main path, so players can weigh them up.
  - The finale fork is obvious.
  - The lighting follows a day cycle, from morning at the start to sunset or night at the end *(sourced)*.
- **Density / scenery:** medium-high. Obstacles are built from deliberate, readable block palettes, with themed dressing around each stage. The path has to stay readable at a glance.
- **Themes (types):** a TV obstacle-course show set in a natural or landscape backdrop that changes per stage.
- **Mood:** focused, personal challenge with a show feel.
- **What players remember:** the time-of-day progression and the three-way fork at the end.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg Parkour Warrior. Confidence: **high** for structure, **low-medium** for lengths.

## Rocket Spleef
- **Rounds / maps:** 3 rounds of 3–4 minutes, from about 12 maps *(sourced)*. The last 2 minutes are a "TNT time" phase *(sourced)*. The later "Rush" variant uses floating platforms that crumble and respawn.
- **Footprint:** one compact arena, about 40–70 blocks across *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** 3–4 stacked, breakable floors about 10–15 blocks apart, with void below *(est.)*. Players carry an elytra and a firework to recover.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - Floors shrink over time and are blown away by rockets.
  - Each floor should be distinct in colour or material, so players know which tier they are on.
  - No safe ledges.
- **Density / scenery:** low-medium. The arena itself is the themed object, such as a giant food, a holiday item or a geometric sculpture. There is little scenery beyond it.
- **Themes (types):** whimsical giant objects, holidays, abstract geometric or sci-fi shapes.
- **Mood:** playful chaos.
- **What players remember:** the silly giant object being blown to pieces.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg Rocket Spleef and mccisland.wiki RSR. Confidence: **medium** (layer counts are estimates).

## Hole in the Wall
- **Rounds / maps:** 3 rounds of 3 minutes, about 10 maps *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** a central platform of about 15–25 square blocks. Walls approach from all directions, starting about 30–40 blocks out *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** walls are about 5–8 tall and made of slime. The backdrop can be very tall, such as buildings or cliffs. Some maps use barrier ceilings.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - The platform shrinks over the round.
  - Wall types get added at set times, and the walls speed up.
  - Each map has its own wall-hole style and platform shape, for example a holey floor or rings.
- **Density / scenery:** the platform is simple and the backdrop is a dense set piece, such as a city skyline, a castle courtyard, an aquarium tank or an ice palace. The surrounding area is decoration only and cannot be stood on.
- **Themes (types):** urban, medieval, aquatic, icy, laboratory, martial-arts or dojo.
- **Mood:** frantic and comedic.
- **What players remember:** the themed backdrop and the wall shapes, not the floor.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg and mccisland.wiki. Confidence: **high** for format.

## Bingo / Lockout (Bingo But Fast)
- **Format:** a 5×5 card, 10 minutes, scored by placement per square plus line bonuses *(sourced)*. Lockout has been both used and removed over time.
- **World:** a survival world rather than a custom-built map. Since the later seasons, each event uses a unique world with a strong theme or geographic feature, such as a big mountain, an island chain or a canyon *(sourced)*. One variant ran in the Nether.
- **Spawn:** a central platform. Players get netherite tools, an elytra, rockets and a teleport pearl *(sourced)*.
- **Density / scenery:** vanilla terrain. The craft is in choosing a seed that has every card item reachable within about 300–600 blocks *(est.)*, and in building a good-looking spawn hub.
- **Mood:** frantic, open-world scramble.
- **What players remember:** the signature landform of each event's world.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg. Confidence: **high**.

## Grid Runners
- **Format:** 10 identical parallel lanes, one per team, each with 8 challenge rooms plus a sprint room. The game runs 12 minutes *(sourced)*. Room selection changes each event, and rooms are built for 4 players.
- **Footprint:** each room is about 20–30 wide and 20–40 deep. With 10 lanes side by side, the whole build is several hundred blocks wide *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** rooms are about 10–20 tall, enclosed, and connected by a door that opens on completion.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - Each room is a self-contained co-op challenge: a puzzle, gathering, combat, parkour or redstone task.
  - The goal must be readable on entry.
  - Lanes are copies, so the build must be modular.
- **Density / scenery:** extremely dense. Every room is a fully dressed themed diorama.
- **Themes (types):** a grab-bag of places in each room (kitchens and bakeries, temples, mills, prisons, sewers, wild west, sports, holiday).
- **Mood:** escape-room teamwork under the clock.
- **What players remember:** the variety of distinct themed rooms.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg Grid Runners. Confidence: **high** for format, **medium** for sizes.

## Railroad Rush
- **Format:** 12 minutes to build the longest minecart track. Copper-door checkpoints are blasted open with TNT carts. There are three 1-minute "gold rush" phases in a hazardous gold cavern *(sourced)*. The best runs reach about 250–385 blocks of track *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** parallel team lanes about 400+ blocks long, crossing terrain obstacles such as stone of graded hardness, leaves, gaps that need bridging, and vault caches *(sourced mechanics)*.
- **Heights:** canyon or terrain relief of 10–40 blocks *(est.)*. The cavern is a separate cave space with lava.
- **Density / scenery:** dense natural terrain plus mining props. A strong outdoor backdrop.
- **Themes (types):** environmental (desert mesa, jungle, snow) plus a spooky variant.
- **Mood:** busy frontier engineering.
- **What players remember:** the landscape and the dash into the gold mine.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg. Confidence: **medium-high**.

## Sands of Time
- **Format:** each team enters its own copy of a temple dungeon. The dungeon is generated per event *(sourced)*, which suggests it is assembled from modular rooms.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - A central hub holds a physical hourglass timer, a "bank" NPC and the exit.
  - Four colour-coded vault branches lead off the hub, each with its own key challenge: a puzzle, a mob gate, a random placement, or lava parkour.
  - Rooms deeper in are worth more *(sourced)*.
  - Cracked walls hide side areas.
- **Footprint:** about 80–150 blocks across per team copy, on 2–4 depth levels *(est.)*.
- **Density / scenery:** extremely dense and dark: torches, traps, sand, gold.
- **Themes (types):** desert tomb or temple. Seasonal re-skins have included snow.
- **Mood:** greedy tension against a ticking sound.
- **What players remember:** the giant hourglass and the coloured vault paths.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg plus a Reddit strategy guide. Confidence: **high** for structure.

## Ace Race
- **Format:** 1–4 laps with a 10–12 minute limit *(sourced)*. Fastest laps run about 1:08–2:17 and fastest full runs about 3:56–7:03 *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** with sprint, boost pads and elytra, a lap is about **800–1500+ blocks of path** *(est.)*. The map is huge.
- **Heights:** tall cliffs, towers or summits feed **elytra glide sections of 50–150 blocks** of drop *(est.)*.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - Checkpoints split the lap into sections and grant or remove items: elytra, riptide trident, dolphin's grace, depth strider *(sourced)*.
  - Section types: running and jumping, speed pads, launch and jump pads, riptide water channels, elytra glides with lift rings, and wind areas.
  - Risky shortcuts. Since one later season, alternate routes change between events *(sourced)*.
- **Density / scenery:** extremely dense and landmark-heavy. The whole lap passes through a complete themed world.
- **Themes (types):** sky or clouds, desert ruins, canyon, city or metro, snow mountain, dark caverns, space.
- **Mood:** grand tour, speed, spectacle.
- **What players remember:** the big glide drops and the signature landmarks along the route.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg Ace Race. Confidence: **high** for mechanics, **medium** for sizes.

## TGTTOS
- **Format:** 6 rounds on 6 different maps, about 90–100 seconds each, from a pool of 40 maps *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** short and straight. About 80–200 blocks from start to finish, and about 20–40 wide so that 40 players fit *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** mostly flat, with gaps, small climbs and drops. Some maps use elytra or boats.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - Start line at one end, finish at the other, where players punch a chicken or fan NPC *(sourced)*.
  - One gimmick per map: bridging, shears, elytra, boats, or player punching.
  - Simple to read.
- **Density / scenery:** medium. Clearly themed, but simple: more like a set than a world.
- **Themes (types):** a wide mix (industrial, zoo, water park, trains, lava, cities, ice).
- **Mood:** quick, silly, comedic.
- **What players remember:** each map's single gimmick.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg and mccisland.wiki. Confidence: **high**.

## Build Mart
- **Format:** 10–12 minutes. A blueprint gallery shows 7 builds that rotate every 90 seconds, plus a rare gold build. Teams work up to 3 builds at a time; the record is 17 *(sourced)*.
- **Versions:** the original was a **giant mall or warehouse interior with departments**. A later redesign used floating categorised resource islands with elytra travel, a central launch fan and speed pads *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** about 200–300 blocks across, with interior walls of **about 80–100+ blocks** and tiered shelving, islands and departments *(est., matches the owner's brief)*.
- **Team area:** a separate build space reached through a portal. It has 3 plots plus 1 gold plot, 5 colour-coded chests, crafting tables, stonecutters and 2 smelting areas *(sourced)*.
- **Density / scenery:** extremely dense. Every material is displayed and the space is full of signage.
- **Mood:** bustling shopping frenzy.
- **What players remember:** the sheer scale and the "store of everything" feel.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg plus a fandom index snippet. Confidence: **high** for structure, **medium** for heights.

## Meltdown
- **Format:** 3 rounds of 5 minutes plus overtime *(sourced)*. Variants: a lab, a quarry-lab and a bastion.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - A grid of square rooms. Team spawn rooms sit on the outer ring, with 5 capture rooms and a central room.
  - Rooms progressively melt into lava floors, then seal behind lowering doors.
  - Striped rooms unlock later.
  - Doors are marked by beams on the walls *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** a grid about 120–180 blocks across, with rooms about 12–20 square and 8–12 tall *(est.)*.
- **Density / scenery:** dense and clean, with colour-coding everywhere.
- **Themes (types):** a clinical lab or industrial facility, occasionally Nether.
- **Mood:** a cold, mechanical squeeze inward.
- **What players remember:** the colour-coded grid and the rooms turning to lava.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg. Confidence: **high** for structure.

## Parkour Tag
- **Format:** 9 rounds of 1 minute, 1 hunter against 3 runners, about 7 maps *(sourced)*. A circular parkour course. Arenas run in parallel.
- **Footprint:** about 30–50 blocks across *(est.)*.
- **Heights:** 3–4 tiers, about 15 tall in total.
- **Structure conventions:**
  - A loopable layout with no dead ends.
  - Several routes up and down.
  - Escape lines balanced around the loop.
- **Density / scenery:** very dense, but obstacles read clearly.
- **Themes (types):** urban, industrial, winter, desert, Asian-inspired townscapes.
- **Mood:** frantic chase.
- **What players remember:** the tight playground loop.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg plus Wikipedia ("circular"). Confidence: **medium**.

## Foot Race
- **Format:** 1 map, 2 laps, 6 minutes. The fastest lap is about 1:26 *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** about 400–600 blocks per lap *(est.)*, with obstacles, pitfalls, risky shortcuts and checkpoints. Players can punch each other.
- **Density / scenery:** medium. The one map was a tomb or temple theme.
- **Mood:** an early-era scramble that became Ace Race. Build it smaller and simpler than Ace Race.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg. Confidence: **medium**.

## Decision Dome
- **Structure conventions:**
  - A central dial divided into up to 8 game segments, with team pods around the rim.
  - Players throw eggs into a segment, and the eggs hatch into chickens that wander, adding randomness.
  - Dividers rise to count the votes.
  - Power-ups recolour or affect segments *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** a dome about 50–70 blocks in diameter, about 25–40 tall *(est.)*.
- **Density / scenery:** medium-high, with a game-show stage look.
- **Mood:** suspenseful game-show reveal.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg. Confidence: **medium-high**.

## Lobby / Hub
- **Structure conventions:**
  - A large island, re-themed each season (a festival park, then a sky island, then a jungle island), with themed holiday re-skins.
  - Zones: the Decision Dome, a hall of fame with a scoreboard, a winners' stage, training mini-arenas for several games, food stalls, parkour, an intro stage and easter eggs *(sourced)*.
- **Footprint:** about 250–400 blocks across, with landmark buildings 30–60 tall *(est.)*.
- **Density / scenery:** extremely dense. It is the showcase map.
- **Mood:** relaxed festival.
- **What players remember:** the hall of fame, the winners' stage and the hidden secrets.
- **Sources / confidence:** wiki.gg Main Hub. Confidence: **high** for features.
