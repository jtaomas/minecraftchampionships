## The main ways real arenas differ
| Structural idea | What it looks like (seen in) | Effect on play |
|---|---|---|
| **Open middle, structure on the edges** | Centre in a wide open courtyard, multi-storey galleries/cell blocks along the sides with balconies (prison-style map); open centre with two side paths and a water elevator per side (lab map, "open centre with two side paths"). | Rushing the centre is visible to everyone; fights happen from the edges and the galleries. |
| **Blocked / enclosed middle** | Centre surrounded by walls, machinery or lava pillars you weave through (lab map with lava pillars round the pad); centre inside an interior hall with mezzanine balconies round it (haunted-house hall). | Close-range fights at the pad; the approach decides it. |
| **Raised centre** | Pad on top of a round tower/plinth reached by stair ramps (graffiti map), on a dark column in an open ruin (lava-ruin map), or raised so it is "not flat on the ground" (festive factory map, wiki text). | Explicitly "discourages the rushing strategy" (wiki); people on the pad are exposed. |
| **Sunken centre / centre in a pit** | Pad down in a pit below sloping hull ramps (spaceship map — "heavily focuses on verticality"). | High ground all around the pad. |
| **Something dividing the halves** | A shallow river across the middle with ships (ships map); an ice sheet over water with an underwater section and a raised mid platform (iceberg map); lava pits you can fall through (lava-ruin map: "open large map ... rushing discouraged"); a train lying across the middle (station map). | Crossing points become the fights; a lower level appears. |
| **One dominant vertical element** | Water elevators that lift you to a top level (festive factory map, lab map) for ranged attacks; tall hull ramps; building rooftops reachable by stairs. | A risky high spot each side. |
| **Corridors / flanks** | Long side corridors around an open centre (prison map; community compared it to a "pipes" layout); hedge/garden walls forming corridors (villa map, video frame). | Flanking routes that hide you from the middle. |
| **Interactive / breakable structure** | Construction blocks you can break (manor map), leaf blocks you can build across (graffiti map), a "direct hole to the centre" for wool rushing (paint factory map), pots with items (bazaar). | Map changes during the round. |
| **Spawn rooms** | Usually glass-gated kit rooms at both ends; some maps use iron bars or stone walls instead, one map's spawn is "blocked off with a 1x2 hole to exit from"; exits open into very different surroundings (straight onto an open floor, into a building, onto a ramp). | Spawn exit shape sets the first 3 seconds. |

Principles that hold for every map seen:
1. Fairness is by **rotation/mirror symmetry of the two halves**; within a half, left and right are often different.
2. Each map has **one big structural idea** (river, hall, pit, tower, open court) — the theme follows from it, not the other way round.
3. Empty space is used on purpose (open courts, open lava floors); not every area is dressed with cover.
4. Verticality comes in one or two clear forms per map (a tower, ramps, galleries, a bridge level), not everywhere.
5. The pad is always readable (hazard frame), whether it is raised, sunken, enclosed or open.

## What we took from it (our own layouts)
- **Greenhouse = enclosed middle**: the pad is inside a glass hall with one door per side; left flank a serpentine hedge
  maze (corridor flank), right flank an open lawn under one tall tower (dominant vertical element). Spawn: two ground doors
  + indoor stair to a roof balcony with a footbridge to the tower.
- **Toy Workshop = open middle + bridge level**: a viaduct crosses the room over the open pad (glass over the pad);
  left flank a tight roofed shelf corridor, right flank an open play-mat with the viaduct stair. Spawn: a long low
  play tunnel + a wide open porch, no front door.
- **Ski Lodge = divided halves**: a frozen ravine wall to wall with a lower ice level, the pad on a mid-river island,
  one bridge and one ice stair onto the island per side; a big lodge on one diagonal, an open slope with a ski-lift tower on
  the other. Spawn: small cabin + wide porch left by its two corners, plus a side door.
- Rejected for now: raised pad on a tower and sunken pad (would need a different dais than the shared one) — good
  candidates for a 4th theme.

### Principles (how the better fan arenas give players options)
1. **Terraced spawn fronts, not a single door** (#1, #8): a wide raised front with several drop/stair points spreads the first push and makes it less predictable.
2. **Three-lane read:** a short exposed centre lane plus two longer covered edge routes (#1).
3. **Mid-level overlook positions** between spawn and centre (#8): ranged players contest the pad without standing on it; reach them by a stair on one side and a slower, exposed ladder on the other, so high ground costs something.
4. **Centre embedded in a frame with partial cover** (#1 ring, #8 sunken courtyard, #3 low walled pit): readable from everywhere, but not open to every angle.
5. **Hazards shape lanes** (#9 lava, #8 cobwebs): a shortcut past a hazard is the risk/reward flank.
6. **Mirror for fairness, theme for identity** (#1); multi-map packs (#2, #3) vary the theme and hazards per map.
7. **Objective state visible at map scale** (#1 recolouring, #3 contrast edge).
8. **Small and dense:** options come from cover density (several low blocks and pillars per quadrant), not size.

### What round 3 took from this (our own layouts)
- Centre embedded in its fixture (cells flush with the hazard ring, lanterns as a frame): principle 4.
- Greenhouse rebuilt around terraced spawn fronts, a three-lane read, mid-level overlooks reached by a stair on one side and a ladder on the other, and a hazard-cut shortcut (principles 1-3, 5).
- Toy Workshop: more than one way on and off the viaduct, mid-level positions under/beside it, a flank with its own risk/reward (principles 2, 3, 5).
