# Parkour Warrior: course structure research

Purpose: understand what makes the courses in MCC's Parkour Warrior look and play the way they do (shape, scale, sections, themes, obstacle construction, readability), so we can design our **own original course** at the same quality. This file covers principles and approximate numbers only. It does not copy an official layout. Official section, obstacle and stage names are deliberately not reused, except as quoted labels in the source notes.

Researched 2026-11-23/24 in the built-in browser. Viewing only: nothing was downloaded and no account was used.

**The main finding:** our generic "three difficulty lanes of plain platforms" attempt missed the core idea. A real course is **one main line of individually designed obstacles**. Each obstacle is a small **themed diorama with one movement idea** and its own name. Jumps are made of **objects** (crates, rails, lanterns, cakes, pipes, tree roots, window sills, hanging props) rather than floating plain blocks. Difficulty choice exists only in two places: the **optional side branches** at each stage hub, and the **three-way final fork**. It is never a set of parallel lanes along the whole course.

## 1. Footprint and overall shape

- **Linear spine with side spurs ("comb" shape).** The main path is one essentially straight line of islands heading away from the start, so the next hub is always visible ahead (S3, S4 rear view). At every stage hub, a **bonus spur leaves to one side** (to the right in the Dojo) and runs as a short line of 3 obstacles. Players do not walk back along it: the scroll teleports them to the junction (S3, S4 "teleported back to the last intersection"). *(High.)*
- **No hub-and-spokes or loops**, and no stacked floors. The **final fork** is the only place where three routes sit side by side: three gates under one pavilion, each leading into its own ending obstacle and its own finish shrine (S4). *(High.)*
- **Length (estimates):**
  - Main sections are roughly 15–35 blocks each (from POV frame spacing and the top-down shots).
  - Hubs are about 12–20 blocks deep.
  - Main spine ≈ 15 × 25 + 5 hubs × 15 + ending ≈ 40 → **about 450–550 blocks from start to finish**.
  - Each bonus spur adds 3 × 20–35 ≈ 60–100 blocks, which is **≈ 300–500 extra blocks** across 5 spurs.
  - The ending obstacles are 30–60 blocks long, and the hard ending also goes up a tall tower.
  - Total built path ≈ 900–1,200 blocks. *(Medium-low; no top-down render exists.)*
- **Overall width:** the spine corridor including scenery is about 30–50 blocks wide, and the spurs extend 60–100 blocks sideways. Background islands sit 20–80 blocks off the line. *(Low-medium.)*

## 2. Completion times

- Best players, MCC, everything (15 main + 15 bonus + hard ending): **≈ 8.5–9.5 min** of the 10:00. In S5, all 15 bonus + hard ending was done with ~1:00 left. *(High.)*
- Per main section for a top player: ≈ 10–20 s. Per bonus section: 15–40 s, plus retries. In the S5 frames, roughly one stage (main + bonus) took 70–110 s. *(Medium.)*
- Dojo (9 main + 9 bonus + hard ending): world-class expert runs ≈ 2:10–3:20 (S8). *(High.)*
- An average player doing main + easy ending only: est. 4–6 min. Taking bonuses is a deliberate trade against finishing. *(Low-medium, inferred from the design intent in S1/S4.)*

## 3. Division into stages and sections

- **5 stages × 3 main sections**, separated by **hub islands** (S1, S4). Each main section is one named obstacle whose floating label reads like "[M2-3] Name" (M = main, stage-section). Bonus obstacles are labelled "[B4-2] Name" (S5, S6). *(High.)*
- Each **bonus spur** has 3 sections and awards bronze → silver → gold in order. Every bonus obstacle has a hanging **EASY / MEDIUM / HARD** sign: the first spur is mostly easy, later spurs get harder (S4 transcript). *(High.)*
- **Ending:** an EASY / MEDIUM / HARD fork, each branch one longer obstacle, each with its own finish (S1, S4). *(High.)*
- **Modular:** Noxcrew said the course "randomises and changes for every event", obstacles are contributed by many designers, and some are **dynamic (timed or rhythmic)** (S4 transcript, S3). The hubs, start and ending architecture are a fixed frame; the obstacles between them are swapped in and out. *(High.)*

## 4. What each section looks like, and what makes it distinct

There are two layers of theming:

1. **Stage (zone) theme.** All three main obstacles of a stage share a biome or material family, and the game says "You completed the X zone!" (S5). Examples: a red-sand/terracotta zone with cacti and rails (S4, S5); a dark stone/deepslate pillar zone (S5); a frosty ice/snow zone (S7); a lush jungle/mangrove zone (S5). The original game ran 8 such zones in sequence. *(High.)*
2. **Obstacle concept.** Inside a zone, each obstacle is its own mini-set with **one movement gimmick and a name**. The name describes either the mechanic or the object: rail tracks, sandy steps, revolving doors, simple neos (S4, S6). Because many designers build them, neighbouring obstacles differ a lot in shape and material, even within one zone. *(High.)*

Observed obstacle sets (paraphrased, from frames):
- **Rails over red sand:** rail lines on terracotta/red sand blocks with cacti; you hop rail-sleeper to rail-sleeper (S4).
- **Hanging chains:** props and cubes hang on chains from an overhead timber canopy (lanterns, crates, little shrine boxes, anvil-like weights), and you jump between the swinging-looking objects (S2, S4).
- **Pillar forest:** tall, thin dark-stone pillars and thin wall fins; jumps go between pillar tops and side ledges, with a vertical drop below (S5).
- **Colossal walls:** huge single-colour slab walls (purple, cyan) as a backdrop and as neo/wall-strafe surfaces (S5, S6).
- **Industrial:** yellow/black hazard-stripe platforms, glass rails, metal blocks (S4, S6).
- **Oversized props:** giant capsule/bottle-like tanks with lamp-posts (S4); giant layered wool cakes (S8); rainbow concrete crayon bars (S8); colour icicles hanging from a ceiling (S6); furnace/blast-furnace rows (S7); lime glass spiral (S7).
- **Organic:** mangrove-root arches with teal-sculpted stone; leaf-covered mossy islands; a frosted tree; snow blocks crumbling away (S5, S7).
- **Building climb (the hard ending):** a tall red-brick building with white window frames and glass; you climb the outside using sills and frames, then come out at the top with a sunset view (S6, S7). The easy and medium endings are lower obstacles ending at the same type of shrine (S4).

## 5. How jumps are built from themed objects (obstacle idea categories)

Kinds of "jump made of a thing" we observed, as categories (not copies):
1. **Hanging objects:** anything on chains (lanterns, crates, cubes, weights, bells), hung from a beam or canopy.
2. **Rails and tracks:** rail lines, sleepers and minecart-track pieces used as narrow footholds.
3. **Architectural fragments:** window sills, frames, balconies, gutters, roof edges and chimneys on a tall façade.
4. **Pillars and posts:** tall thin columns, fence posts, lamp-posts, bollards; landing on 1×1 tops.
5. **Giant everyday props:** cakes, crayons/markers, bottles/tanks, barrels, books, stacked furniture, at 3–10× scale.
6. **Natural shapes:** roots, branches, mushroom caps, cactus tops, icicles, boulders, coral.
7. **Machinery:** furnaces, pipes, conveyor-like stripes, hazard-striped catwalks, pistons, trapdoors.
8. **Wall-strafe / neo surfaces:** big flat walls or slab fins you jump around (the "neo").
9. **Spirals and stairs:** glass helix, staircase fragments, ladder pieces, scaffolding.
10. **Ladder/vine catches:** climbable strips placed so you jump onto them mid-air.
11. **Ice and slip surfaces:** packed/blue ice runs, snow layers.
12. **Dynamic/rhythm pieces:** timed doors/blocks that appear, disappear or rotate ("revolving doors"), and crumbling blocks (S4 transcript, S6, S7).
13. **Head-hitters and ceilings:** low overhangs under canopies that force tight timing.
14. **Drops and dives:** controlled falls onto a lower landing, past diagonal beams (S7).

**Rule of thumb from the frames:** the *walkable* parts are 1–3 blocks wide and made of the prop's own material. The prop is much bigger than the parts you stand on, so it reads as an object, not a platform.

## 6. Verticality

- The spine stays roughly level, with gentle rises and falls of ±5–10 blocks between hubs *(medium)*.
- Individual obstacles use height: towers and tall pillars go **10–35 blocks up** (hard-ending tower ≈ 25–35 tall, est.), and some include dives back down.
- Void or sky lies below everything. A fall of 5–30 blocks ends in the void and **respawns you at the last checkpoint** ("You fell! Returning to last checkpoint", S4/S5).
- Background islands float at varied heights, both above and below the path, for depth.

## 7. Checkpoints, hubs, start and finish

- **Checkpoint after every obstacle:** a small landing island/deck. The chat says "Spawn point updated". In the Dojo the pad is a purple square on a wooden deck (S8). On landing, a stone medal (main) or a bronze/silver/gold medal (bonus) pops up as "+1" (S5, S6).
- **Stage hubs (5):** larger islands in fixed east-Asian dojo architecture:
  - Pagoda/torii frames: red pillars, teal roofs.
  - Red lanterns, stone lanterns, iron railings, planted shrubs.
  - A **stone frog statue** as the junction marker.
  - A **"BONUS PATH" banner sign** pointing to the side spur (S4).
  - A village-street hub variant has wooden decks, houses either side, a sign gate and hanging chains (S5, S6).
- **Start:** a pagoda island with the event banners. Everyone starts together.
- **Final fork:** an open timber pavilion with a teal roof. It has three entrances, each with a large hanging framed sign (EASY with 1 star in lime-on-green, MEDIUM with 2 stars in orange, HARD with 3 stars in red) and **colour-matched pillars**. Braziers sit on teal plinths, and there is TNT (S4).
- **Finish:** each ending stops at a small shrine: a portal set in a white quartz frame up a short stone/wood stair, flanked by thick pillars **in the route's colour** (lime / yellow / red). A **golden frog statue** marks the finish (S4). The sky is at sunset by then.

## 8. How the path is made readable

- A **straight spine** with the next hub always visible on the horizon (S4 rear view).
- A **floating label** at each obstacle start (code + name), and big hanging signs for difficulty.
- **Contrast:** footholds are warm, saturated or light materials (planks, terracotta, gold, coloured concrete, quartz) against grey/dark structure, void and sky. Big single-colour walls frame the line.
- **Colour language:** lime / orange-yellow / red = easy / medium / hard, repeated on signs, pillars and finish portals.
- **HUD:** a progress strip across the top with one diamond per section (bonus sections in purple), a timer, medals and a team multiplier (S4, S5).
- **Day cycle:** the sky moves from morning to sunset, so the light itself shows how far along you are. One event (MCC 30) used an orange sky throughout (S1, S4, S5).

## 9. Scenery, backdrop and detail density

- **Medium-high density**, concentrated on the path: every obstacle is dressed (plants, lanterns, chains, signs) and hubs are dense. Between obstacles there is open sky. *(High.)*
- **Backdrop:** floating islands and tall decorative columns (thick coloured pillars like giant posts: teal, red, purple, lime) at 20–80 blocks, distant pagoda silhouettes, a low sun. There is no ground plane. *(Medium-high.)*
- **Palette of the fixed frame:** dark oak/spruce wood, red pillars, teal (prismarine/warped) roofs and trims, stone bricks, red lanterns, cherry pink accents. Each zone's obstacles then bring their own palette.

## 10. Spectators and multiple players

- **One shared course.** All 40 players start together and run the same line (S9 original; S4/S5/S6 show several players on the same obstacle). The other players' name tags are visible. *(High.)*
- **No player collision:** players overlap at hubs and on 1-block footholds in the frames, with no pushing visible. *(Medium; not stated in text.)*
- **Spectator views:** the broadcast uses free-cam shots: top-down over the spine, a long rear view, close-ups at the fork (S4). Where finished players go is not shown. *(Low.)* Per `LESSONS.md`, use spectator mode with an overview teleport.

## 11. Bonus and harder branches

- **Side spur at each of the 5 hubs:** 3 obstacles marked easy/medium/hard. The first spur is mostly easy; the later ones are harder. They pay bronze/silver/gold medals in order. You leave at any time with a teleport scroll back to the junction (S1, S4). *(High.)*
- **Final fork:** easy / medium / hard. A harder finish gives a bigger team multiplier. You can switch until the last 30 s (S1). *(High.)*
- **No hidden shortcuts or secret routes** were seen. *(Medium.)*

## Design requirements for our own course

1. **Shape:** one straight main spine of about 450–550 blocks (start → 5 hubs → final fork). Put a bonus spur of 3 obstacles (60–100 blocks) off **one side** of each hub. Replace our current three parallel lanes entirely.
2. **Units:** 5 stages × 3 main obstacles, plus 5 spurs × 3 bonus obstacles, plus 3 ending obstacles, for **33 individually designed obstacles**. Each one is 15–35 blocks long (endings 30–60).
3. **One idea per obstacle:** each obstacle has a single movement gimmick, a name and a floating label (for example `[M3-2] Name`, `[B3-2] Name`). No two obstacles in a row should share the same gimmick.
4. **Objects, not platforms:** every foothold is part of a recognisable themed object that is clearly bigger than the foothold. Plain floating cubes are allowed only as rare connectors.
5. **Zone theme per stage:** all 3 main obstacles of a stage, and that stage's spur, share one material/biome family. The theme changes at each hub, and a "zone complete" message shows when it does.
6. **Fixed frame architecture:** start, hubs, fork pavilion and finish shrines share one consistent original architectural style (ours, not pagodas). Each hub has a mascot statue at the junction and a big sign pointing to the bonus spur.
7. **Difficulty colour language:** lime / orange / red signs, pillars and finish shrines mark easy / medium / hard. Every bonus obstacle gets a hanging difficulty sign. The first spur is easy, and the spurs get harder towards the last one.
8. **Checkpoints:** a small, distinct landing pad after every obstacle (one material used only for pads), with a sound/title cue and a medal popup. A fall below the obstacle's lowest point returns the player to the last pad.
9. **Spur exit:** an item that teleports the player back to the junction. On finishing a spur, return them to the junction automatically.
10. **Verticality budget:** spine within ±10 blocks; individual obstacles may climb up to 15–35 (a tower) or dive. Keep 5–30 blocks of void under every foothold.
11. **Readability:** a straight line with the next hub visible. Footholds in light/warm or saturated materials against darker structure and sky; nothing decorative at foothold height that could be mistaken for a foothold.
12. **Movement vocabulary to cover across the course:** gaps of 1–4, up-jumps, drops, neos/wall-strafes, ladder/vine catches, 1×1 posts, head-hitters, slime or ice, and at least 2–3 **timed/rhythm** obstacles (blocks that appear/disappear or doors on a cycle).
13. **Difficulty ramp:** main obstacles go from easy (stage 1) to medium-hard (stage 5). The hard ending should be clearly the hardest thing on the map; the easy ending should be finishable by anyone.
14. **Endings:** three side-by-side gates under one pavilion. Each leads to a distinct ending obstacle (the hard one a tall climb) and to its own coloured finish shrine with a big mascot statue.
15. **Scenery:** dress every obstacle and hub, keep open sky between them, and place backdrop islands and tall decorative columns 20–80 blocks off the line. No ground plane.
16. **Day cycle:** run the in-game time from morning at the start to sunset at the end of the 10 minutes.
17. **Shared course:** all players start on one wide start island and run the same line, with player collision off. Spectators get spectator mode with overview teleports (top-down over the spine, the fork).
18. **Timing check:** test that a strong player can do everything (main + all bonuses + hard) in about 8.5–9.5 min, and that an average player can do main + easy in 4–6 min.

## Original section-theme ideas (ours, for the 5 stages plus spares)

1. **Clockwork workshop:** brass/copper gears as round platforms (some rotate on a timer); pendulum weights on chains; escapement teeth as 1×1 steps; giant springs (slime) as bounces; a cuckoo-clock façade with doors that open on the beat.
2. **Giant's breakfast table:** stacked pancakes (layered terracotta), a sugar-cube trail, spoon handles as narrow beams, an overturned cup with a neo around its rim, cereal-loop rings to jump through, a butter block on ice-slick plates.
3. **Laundry line over a canyon:** clothes on long lines (carpet/banner "shirts" as hanging footholds), clothes-peg posts, a wicker basket climb, a soap-bubble field of glass spheres, a vine ladder catch onto a hanging towel.
4. **Shipwreck in the sky:** a broken mast as a balance beam, crow's-nest jumps, hanging cargo nets (ladders), a barrel scatter, cannon tops as 1×1 posts, a rope bridge with planks missing, the ribs of the hull as a pillar run.
5. **Library tower:** giant book spines as stepped platforms (each book a different colour), open-book ramps, a rolling-ladder catch along a shelf, inkwell posts, hanging chandeliers, a spiral staircase with missing treads; a good fit for the hard climb.
6. **Beehive and meadow:** honeycomb hex columns at different heights, stalk-and-bloom flower posts, a honey-block slow section, a dripping-honey timed path, a leaf-blade wall strafe, a hollow log tunnel with head-hitters.
7. **Crystal mine:** amethyst cluster footholds, minecart-rail beams on trestles, timbered supports as a pillar forest, glowing lantern chains in a dark cavern, a lift cage with timed platforms, a dripstone stalactite descent.
8. **Toy box:** giant building bricks with studs (button/slab tops), a spinning-top spiral, domino chain posts, a jack-in-the-box bounce, a toy train track on stilts, stacked alphabet blocks.
9. **Frozen harbour:** ice floes (blue ice) with slide jumps, a moored-boat hop, a lighthouse climb for a tower obstacle, frozen fishing nets as ladders, icicle-fringed bridge rails as head-hitters.
10. **Market rooftops:** awnings as bouncy or striped platforms, hanging shop signs on chains, a fruit-crate stack, chimney posts, a laundry pole run between houses, gutter-and-drainpipe wall climbs.

Spares let the coordinator pick 5 and keep the rest for spurs or a later course rotation (the real game reshuffles per event).
