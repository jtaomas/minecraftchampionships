# TGTTOS (To Get To The Other Side) - map structure research (Sonnet agent)

Scope: structure and principles only, for original maps on a private fan server. Official map names are avoided; maps are referred to by a descriptor (for example "honeycomb map"). Confidence tags: H high, M medium, L low.

## 0. Evidence base
- Wiki (mcchampionship.wiki.gg, game page TGTTOSAWAF): 40 maps in the infobox, 34 current and 13 event-exclusive in the lists. Six rounds on six different maps, 1:30 to 1:40 per round. Objective: reach the far end and whack an NPC "fan" (earlier a chicken). Players may punch each other off. Rewards: placement coins, plus team bonuses for the first four teams. Remixes include reversed courses (finish to start), a stacking variant and chicken-riding. Per-map wiki pages are nearly text-free (one sentence each); the images are the real source.
- Wiki images viewed full-size (1920x1080 promo shots, mostly taken at the START area looking toward the course): 18 maps (https://mcchampionship.wiki.gg/images/TGTTOS-<Name>.png for Badlands, Doors, Walls, Boats, Pits, Glide, SkydiveOriginal, Beehive, Zoo, Industry, Spiral, Siege, Golf, Domino, SlipperySlope, Skyscraper, Launcher, Water_Park, TrainPassing, Maze, IceMountain, Cliff).
- mccisland.wiki/TGTTOS (Island version): 34 maps, default round 90 s, a time-skip (timer drops to 20 s once 80 percent finish), finish is a chicken pit; some maps allow block placing.
- YouTube (view only): Route Guide video aYOv_Vr0Iyc (Island version, expert POV, 26 map segments, 777 s total, roughly 20 to 45 s per map, labelled in-frame). Frames sampled at 0:24 (dome map), 0:46 (castle), 1:15 (drop-zone), 1:50 and 2:15 (funhouse), 2:40/2:42 (train), 3:05 (industry), 3:28 (honeycomb), 3:52 (canyon), 4:18 (cliff), 4:35 (skyscraper low-gravity), 7:50 (skydive), 8:45 (doors), 9:10 (bricks), 9:45 (chilli/food). Also "Fastest Completion of every TGTTOSAWAF Map" tB8y6pw9PUo (778 s for ~40 maps, ~19 s average = the record crossing time; POV frames were low value).
- Caveat: the browser pane tab was shared with another agent for part of the session (tab switching), so several video seeks were lost. Total looked at: about 22 distinct maps, 33 images/frames.

## 1. Footprint and timing
- Length start to goal: about 60-200 blocks, most maps 80-150 (M, estimated from the promo shots and from record times of 20-40 s at sprint/bridge speed; sprint is about 5.6 b/s, speed-bridging about 4-5 b/s, so 20-35 s implies 100-170 blocks of effective path). Elytra/glide and boat maps are longer in blocks but quicker.
- Width: the playable lane is narrow, often 5-15 blocks wide in the bridging maps, and 25-40 where 40 players start side by side (M). The path never needs more width than that; the rest is void or scenery.
- Height: usually 20-60 blocks of vertical range; a couple of "tower" maps are mainly vertical (about 80-120 blocks) (M-L).
- Crossing time: record runs 20-40 s; typical player 45-80 s; round cap 90-100 s with the first finisher usually at 25-45 s. A crossing is therefore a sprint, not a journey (H).
- Design target: records near 25 s, median finisher 50-60 s, cap 90 s, and a time-skip when most have finished.

## 2. Start and goal
- Start: a wide launch platform/pen at one end (castle courtyard, stadium floor, steps, boat pier, rooftop, a gate with sandy floor). Several promo shots show the whole field of players standing shoulder-to-shoulder in rows on a flat deck of about 25-40 wide by 8-15 deep (H). The start is usually enclosed on three sides (walls, buildings, cliff) so nobody starts next to the void; the open side faces the course.
- Goal: a mirror-image deck/gate at the far end with the NPC "fans" lined up or on a podium, with a MCC-style arch/banner (red-white candy-striped arch visible from the start in an ice map; striped checkpoint arches; bunting). Visible from the start in nearly every map: the start shots always show the finish arch or a themed landmark on the horizon (H). Exceptions: tower/skyscraper maps, where you see the building face but not the roof.
- Goal marker: a banner/arch plus the NPC crowd; hazard stripes (yellow/black) on industrial maps; flags in themed colours.

## 3. Categories of map (all seen in at least one map)
1. Speed-bridging over void (most common). Players get a stack of blocks (coloured; wool/concrete-like) and a thin, sometimes winding void gap of ~60-120 blocks, often with a few pre-built floating islands as rest points. Variants: open void; with obstacles to bridge around (cobwebs, guardians, water); with floating coloured cube clusters (skydive: bridge between large coloured platform cubes, from the last yellow platform to the last blue one); a straight "bricks" wall run; vertical towers where you bridge up.
   - Fun: skill ceiling, risk/reward of shortest line, visible leaders, void kills (respawn at start or checkpoint).
2. Pre-built obstacle course ("parkour/gauntlet", no block placing). Industrial/construction/carnival: moving conveyors, hanging platforms, ramps, ladders, slime/honey, bounce pads, rainbow ramps, fun-house rooms. Structure is a sequence of 3-6 distinct rooms/segments, each with its own trick.
3. Boats/water (ice-boats, long water channel, water-park flumes). Boat on ice for speed, with a tight lane; water park: slides, pools, trampolines with a candy-striped course.
4. Elytra / glide / launch. A launcher or fan at the start flings players; elytra through rings or between floating coloured pillars; low gravity variants. Crossing is mostly airtime with 2-3 steering decisions.
5. Doors / luck / maze-like choice. A long hall with rows of doors where about 2 in 5 open on each row (caption in the route guide), or a hedge/cliff maze. Fun: shared knowledge is useless; nearby players reveal paths (and block them).
6. Push / PvP crossings. Narrow bridge or ledge, everyone shoulder to shoulder, knock others into the void (stairs/trains with moving platforms). The wiki confirms players can punch each other; there is a karma system that reduces knockback for the player punched most.
7. Climb/vertical (tall building facade, ice mountain, spiral tower, rooftop). Players ascend via blocks, ladders, stairs in windows or a spiral of ramps; finish is the roof.
8. Drop/descent maps. A hole or shaft down; land on the right target (drop-zone map: a target-square floor, last drop must land on the nearest block).
9. Dig/wall crossings. The "walls" family (a wall or maze of thin barriers with cobwebs, guardians, water) where you place or break to get through.
- Share across maps (estimate over about 30 maps): bridging around 35 percent, pre-built obstacle gauntlet around 30 percent, boats/water 10 percent, elytra/launch 8 percent, door/maze/luck 7 percent, climb 7 percent, others 3 percent (L-M, by impression, not counted).

## 4. Scenery vs gameplay, decoration, themes
- Gameplay path is a thin ribbon; scenery is mostly distant: a themed set (sketchy "slice" cross-section style) rather than a world. About 80 percent of visible volume in a typical promo shot is set dressing (buildings, trees, islands, signs); gameplay volume (path) is maybe 10-20 percent (M).
- Theme drives palette and a big landmark per map: castle/siege courtyard (grey stone, red banners, wooden drawbridge doors, moat and trees), honeycomb (orange/yellow comb cells on giant branches, bees, brown honey bumps), desert/badlands (terracotta orange with red-and-white goal flags), ice mountain (white/blue ice, icicles, wooden hut, spruce), city/construction (brick towers with MCC signs, yellow/black hazard stripes, "keep out" gate, small park blocks at the base), skyscraper (a front facade with lit windows, red bricks), stadium/colosseum (sandy floor, red-roofed turrets, hanging red banners), tropical water park (white/coloured concrete, pagodas, palms, red-white bunting), zoo (stone cliff, wooden fences, white tents), lava/canyon (netherrack, red lava, grey stone), train/overpass (floating grass islands, red train carriages, rainbow-coloured concrete poles), golf (checkerboard grass floating islands, red flags), rainbow dominoes (coloured wool rows standing like dominoes).
- Detail density: medium. Large flat colour surfaces and simple big shapes; heavy silhouettes (a castle, a skyscraper); stock texture work is limited. The owner memory of "fairly straight and simple" is confirmed for the path, but the surroundings are clearly built and themed (H).
- Readability tools: bright saturated team-agnostic colours for blocks, big signs, striped hazard areas, flags marking the finish, and distinct colour per section.
- Void/walls: most are floating islands in an empty sky with a plain blue sky box; where there is no void (stadium, castle, zoo), tall themed walls enclose the field. No glass galleries. Sky is a daylight blue with low fog; sometimes desert horizon colour.

## 5. How 40 players fit
- Start deck of about 30 x 10, or two-three tiers (stairs/steps) giving each player a 1x1 slot with a spawn spread; the next row stands behind. Most promo shots show a tight crowd on a flat deck, and at the end an NPC crowd of equal size.
- Early congestion is deliberate: the first 5-10 s are a scrum; then the pack stretches. Lanes widen on bridging maps by letting each player place their own line (void is wide at the start, narrows later), so 40 players do not need 40 pre-built lanes.
- Choke points exist on pre-built maps (a door, a ladder, a ramp) and cause the fun/overtaking moments, but are never longer than a few blocks.

## 6. Differentiation (why they are not reskins)
- Each map has one gimmick/vehicle/movement mode, and the gimmick changes the geometry: horizontal vs vertical, linear vs branching, void vs solid. Visible variety axes: movement verb (place, run, ride, glide, fall, climb, push), terrain (void, water, ice, lava, interior), scale (tall towers vs long strips), theme and colour (warm desert vs cold ice vs neon rainbow vs grey stone), start orientation (courtyard facing the course vs rooftop looking down), and variant (halloween, festive, pride re-skins of the same map exist for events).
- Sequence of six rounds gets different verbs: good rotation avoids two of the same category back to back.

## 7. Confidence summary
- H: round format, objective, time budget, start crowd layout, finish visible from start, one gimmick per map, themed but simple.
- M: lengths/widths/heights, percentages of categories, scenery ratio.
- L: exact counts of obstacles; palette percentages.

## 8. Design requirements for our own maps
1. Straight-line course: one start deck, one goal deck, 80-160 blocks apart; a bend is allowed but the finish should be visible (or hinted) from the start.
2. Playable width 6-15 blocks on the course, 25-40 blocks wide on the start deck; nothing wider except the scenery behind.
3. Crossing time target: 25-35 s for an expert, 45-70 s for a median player, 90 s round cap; add a time-skip (20 s) when 80 percent have finished.
4. Start deck fits 40 players on distinct 1x1 slots, rows 2-3 deep, enclosed on the back and sides; the first 5 s should be a controlled scrum, not a fall.
5. Goal deck mirrors the start: a striped arch/banner plus a row of NPC fans at the end (finish by punching an NPC); visible from the start.
6. Exactly one gimmick per map (place blocks, ride, glide, climb, doors, push, drop, dig). Do not stack three mechanics.
7. Rotate categories: never two maps of the same verb in a row; keep 8-10 categories in the pool (see section 9).
8. Scenery is distant and themed; the path is readable in 1 s: contrast between path blocks and background, no look-alike decoys unless the map is about decoys.
9. Void drops respawn at start or last checkpoint within 2 s; no combat death spirals; void is the only hazard on bridging maps.
10. Blocks given on bridging maps in a stack of about 64-128 coloured blocks per player, refilled once (or infinite with a cooldown); keep the stack count visible.
11. A rest point (a floating island or shelf) every 25-40 blocks on bridging maps so the long void does not feel endless.
12. Punching/knockback is allowed on the push and narrow-path maps only; keep a karma-style knockback scaling so one player cannot dominate.
13. Per map: one big landmark silhouette (castle, tower, hive, mountain) visible from the start; saturated palette of 3-5 hues plus neutral grey.
14. No glass galleries; spectators are in spectator mode with a free-fly camera limited to the arena.
15. Hazard stripes and flags to mark obstacles and the finish; large pixel-style signage for theme and direction.
16. Pre-built obstacle maps: 3-6 segments, each with its own trick and a clear transition; choke points of at most 3-5 blocks long.
17. Vertical maps get their own height (60-120 blocks) but stay narrow (8-12 wide) so 40 players cannot all fit and must queue/overtake.
18. Add 2-3 themed variants (night, snow, festival) of the same geometry later; do not write 40 maps first. Start with 6-8 distinct originals.

## 9. Original map concepts (each a different category)
1. **Sky-Orchard Bridge (speed bridging).** A void ribbon of about 120 blocks with six floating orchard islands as rest points; the void narrows from 40 to 12 wide; an apple-tree landmark. Scenery: warm autumn palette. Fun: pick the safe line vs the shortest line; pre-placed hanging fruit crates as optional one-way shortcuts.
2. **Frostline Boat Run (boats on ice).** A winding ice lane 6 wide, 150 long, with narrow ramps and two gates of packed-ice pylons; boats start in a tray. Fun: drifting, bumping into others; ice cracks to a water pool if you fall.
3. **Clockwork Gauntlet (pre-built obstacle course).** Five rooms: conveyor, swinging pistons, rotating bars, trapdoor floor, slime ramps; yellow/black hazard stripes; a steam-organ landmark. Fun: timing and route choice per room; no block placing.
4. **Turbine Glide (elytra/launch).** A launcher at the start flings players into a wind tunnel of three rings; ring colours show the correct line; a landing platform with pads. Fun: steering, boosting through rocket pads, bumping into other flyers.
5. **Hall of Doors (luck + shared info).** A 90-block corridor in a library with eight rows of three doors, two of which open; a vertical ladder at the end. Fun: social inference; doors reset at half time; players can hold doors for others or close them.
6. **Narrow Bridge Brawl (push/PvP).** A 3-wide stone bridge 70 blocks long between two cliffs with three wider bays (stairs and loops), a sweeping wind-gust every 15 s; start crowd shoulder to shoulder. Fun: pushing, with karma scaling.
7. **Spire Ascent (vertical climb).** A 100-block-high lighthouse; an outer spiral of ramps, window ledges with ladders, and short ladders between landings; the goal at the top lantern. Fun: overtaking at choke landings; falls land on a netted ledge, losing only a bit of time.
8. **Pit Descent (drop/landing).** Players start at the top of a huge shaft; each level has coloured landing pads and holes, with the last target a bullseye floor; the correct sequence of coloured holes shown on signs. Fun: drop-chaining and aim.
9. **Dig-Through Ruins (walls to break/place).** A series of four thick sandstone walls with a pickaxe and shovel given to players; each wall has a weak vein of soft blocks (gravel and sand) as a visible route and hard blocks around. Fun: dig tunnels, see others' tunnels, backfill gravel traps.
10. **Carousel Park (moving-platform course).** A fairground with three carousels (rotating platforms), a swinging pirate-ship platform and a ferris-wheel gondola ride; rainbow bunting. Fun: timing of moving platforms, catches and misses; all moving elements run on tick-based cycles.
